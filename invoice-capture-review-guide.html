How to Build an Invoice Capture and Review Workflow

A useful invoice workflow makes three things clear: what arrived, what was checked and what happened next. Start with one document type and one accounting destination.

Nexus editorial team · 26 November 2026

1. Decide where invoices enter the workflow

Choose the approved intake channel and decide who monitors it. Keep the original file attached to its record. If several teams handle the same supplier, agree who owns intake so copies do not become separate transactions.

2. Separate extraction from approval

Use extracted fields as a draft. The reviewer checks the supplier, invoice reference, invoice date, currency, net and tax amounts, and total against the original. If the destination needs a line-level breakdown, check quantities, unit prices and any rounding before handover.

3. Give exceptions a clear owner

  • Possible duplicate: Check supplier and reference first, then compare currency and total. Do not discard the document based on one field alone.
  • Unreadable or incomplete scan: Keep it out of the ready queue and ask the submitter for a better copy.
  • Unexpected bank details: Pause and verify the change through an established supplier contact, not the contact details on the incoming document.
  • Credit note: Keep it distinct from an invoice and confirm the intended document type before handover.

4. Record the handover result

Mark the record as complete only when the intended destination accepts it. Record the resulting reference where available. If the result is unclear, check the destination before retrying; a retry is not evidence that the first attempt failed.

5. Run a small pilot before expanding

Choose a representative batch with normal invoices, duplicates and a poor scan. Write down how many needed manual corrections and where they stalled. Use the results to agree reviewer responsibilities and decide whether the process is ready for more document types.

Worked example: The same invoice arrives twice

Suppose a supplier emails invoice INV-1048 for £240 and a colleague uploads a photo of the same invoice. The reviewer compares the supplier, reference, currency and total, then checks whether the invoice already exists in the destination system. If it was already accepted, record the destination reference and keep the second submission out of the handover queue. If the documents differ, hold both for review rather than treating either one as a duplicate automatically.

For your pilot, log the intake channel, exception reason, reviewer, decision and destination reference. That gives the team a record of why the invoice moved forward or stayed on hold. The supplier invoice and amount in this example are fictional.
